Reading Guide · Atmospheric Re-Entry

Re-Entry Reading Guide

Learn how spacecraft slow down, manage heat, and descend safely through the atmosphere with clear visual ideas before running flight labs.

Open Lab Back Home
01 · The Basics

What happens during re-entry?

A spacecraft in orbit moves fast. Coming back safely means trading that high speed into controlled drag and heat protection as it meets the upper air.

01 · High Speed

Orbital velocity is huge

Before descending, a spacecraft carries massive energy from moving sideways around Earth—not just from altitude alone.

02 · Air Drag & Heat

Air slows the craft and warms the shield

As thin upper air compresses in front of the craft, drag slows it down while heat builds up along the entry face.

03 · Entry Angle

Too shallow or too steep matters

A balanced entry corridor keeps heating and deceleration manageable instead of bouncing off or diving in too hard.

02 · Heat Shields & Braking

How heat shields protect the craft

Most of a spacecraft's energy is turned into heat in the upper atmosphere. A heat shield keeps that heat away from the cabin while air drag does the heavy braking.

Focused Concept · 01

A wide, blunt face pushes hot air ahead

Instead of slicing sharply like a dart, a rounded heat shield creates a shock layer in front of the craft so much of the heat stays in the air rather than pouring straight into the hull.

Energy trade-off

Speed drops step by step as air resistance converts motion into heat and sound.

Safe inner cabin

Insulation and ablative layers protect the structure until speeds are low enough for final descent.

A short backward engine burn lowers the path
